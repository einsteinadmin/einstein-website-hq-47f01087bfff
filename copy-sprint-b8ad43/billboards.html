Billboards: punchier concepts wanted.

The bank we have is solid. It's also polite. You're the person we call when polite isn't the assignment.

Why this one has a clock

A fall DFW campaign is locked: 5 Lamar panels, 16 weeks, Aug 31 – Dec 20. Artwork gets decided by ~Aug 25 — so new concepts need to land within about two weeks to make this buy.

Anything that misses the window isn't wasted — it goes into the bank for the next buy. But the fun version of this is your line on a Dallas highway by Labor Day.

The ask

Punchier billboard concepts — headline-level only. No layouts, no body copy. (There is no body copy. It's a billboard.)

  1. 6–10 options. Quantity over polish — we'll pick and mock.
  2. Seven words or fewer each. A driver at 65mph gives you half a read.
  3. The over-commit-then-deadpan register is very welcome here. That's the muscle we can't fake in-house.
  4. If a line needs a visual to land, describe it in a parenthetical — on a board, the image is half the sentence. Our mascot (illustrated Einstein) is the standing hero.

The approved bank Locked

How to read this: your new concepts compete with these — they don't replace them. This bank is what runs if nothing beats it.

Micro-attribution "— Dallas Morning News" under the headline. Visual: mascot holding a giant "5." No URL, no phone — the restraint is the brand.

B2 — competitive jab

"55 awards. Zero hidden fees."

B3 — reviews flex

"Other movers hide their reviews. We frame ours."

The 4.9★ and the Dallas move count render as art — a framed review in the mascot's hands — not as copy.

B4 — local personality

"Over 10,000 Dallas moves. We could do yours blindfolded. (We won't.)"

B5 — awards visual

"55 awards. We're running out of wall."

Mascot placing award badges. Conditional: runs only if the badge art unmistakably reads as a moving company's awards — if "awards for what?" survives the design mock, we fall back to B1.

Rules that bind any new line

  1. The pricing tripwire. We run variable pricing — weekday and weekend rates differ by design. Whole-cost claims are safe ("no hidden fees," "the price we quote is the price you pay"). Anything implying one unchanging price is now a lie ("the price is the price," "no surge, ever"). Test every price-flavored line: does it promise the whole cost, or one unchanging price?
  2. The issuer rides along. Any award or "voted best" claim names who said it, on the same board ("— Dallas Morning News"). An award without an issuer is a boast; with one it's a citation.
  3. Market numbers keep their label. Dallas counts stay "Dallas" (10,000+ moves = Dallas; 100k+ = company-wide). A market number never drifts company-wide, and vice versa — even when "Dallas" feels like the first word to cut. It always is. Don't.
  4. True-by-targeting claims don't travel. Our mailers say "$0 trip/fuel fee" — true only because of where we mail them. That class of claim never goes on a board.
  5. Written with the visual. Mascot as hero, illustration only — no photography, no QR codes. One image = one idea.

Retired & killed Don't re-pitch

"This ain't our first rodeo."

Retired. Regional wallpaper — every roofer, realtor, and rib joint in DFW is already renting it.

"The most awarded movers in Dallas."

Killed as a bare superlative — nobody has verified "most," and we can't. We own the count (55) and the streak; we don't own "most." Weld a number on or leave it.

"Alright alright alright."

Killed on the McConaughey trademark question. Not worth the lawyer letter, however Texan it felt.

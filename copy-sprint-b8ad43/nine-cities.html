The other nine cities: local color, nine ways.

The Locations template is built. What it can't fake is knowing the neighborhood — that part gets written once per city, by you.

The setup

The North Austin Locations page is the model for all ten city pages, and its best trick is the local flavor: "first homes in Pflugerville," the "third-floor walk-up in Hyde Park," schools filling the summer calendar because of Round Rock ISD. That's the part that makes the page read like it was written by someone who lives there — because the copy sweats the details only a local would.

The template handles everything else. But each of the other nine cities needs its own version of those localized components, and a find-and-replace on "Austin" would be exactly the generic mush the model was built to kill.

The ask — per city, roughly six slots

Read the North Austin model once, then write these for each city. Every one has a marked slot in the template, so "short and specific" beats "long and flowing":

  1. Hero localities line — the "Pflugerville to The Domain" span. Two endpoints locals would nod at.
  2. Service-card local flavor — the residential + apartment blurbs ("first homes in Pflugerville," "third-floor walk-up in Hyde Park"). One real detail each.
  3. Neighborhood one-liners — a sentence per neighborhood in the coverage grid ("fast-growing, family-heavy, full of our repeat customers").
  4. Local tips — 2–4 bullets: schools, traffic quirks, the thing everyone racing a move-in date needs to know.
  5. The wink — one Bird-Bird-Biscuit-grade local joke or reference per city. Just one; it has to be real.
  6. Long-distance geography — where people in that city actually move to and from. Tampa's card can't say "anywhere in the Lone Star State."

Division of labor, so numbers stay honest: we supply the locked per-city facts — award names and counts, review numbers, since-years, manager names, neighborhood lists. You supply the color. Never both from the same pen. If a fact slot is empty when you get there, write around it with a {{placeholder}} and keep moving. And if a city stumps you — you've never set foot in Garland, fair — write what research gives you and flag the gaps; the branch teams can feed you local intel.

Sending it back

One Google Doc with nine sections (or nine short docs — dealer's choice), slots labeled 1–6 per city, to cameron@einsteinmoving.com. Cities can trickle in as they finish — no need to hold for the full set.
